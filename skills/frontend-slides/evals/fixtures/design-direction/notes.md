# Talk notes — Is Simpler Always Better?

- Format: 6-slide talk, 12 minutes, English.
- Density: speaker-led; one idea per slide, large type, short phrases.
- Audience: second-year design students; most know Dieter Rams only as "the Braun guy".
- Occasion: design history seminar, followed by discussion.

## Thesis

Simplicity is not a visual style. It is the discipline of removing what does
not serve the user. Rams's "less but better" is a standard for decisions, not
a palette.

## Flow

1. Title: "Is Simpler Always Better?"
2. The promise: Rams and the Braun era; why "less" became a virtue.
3. The counter-case: a door with a push/pull sign, a remote with too many
   buttons, settings menus. Complexity moves the cost to the user.
4. The test: can someone use it without instructions? Does every control
   earn its place?
5. Simplicity has a cost: removing the wrong thing hides necessary choices.
6. Close: simplicity is an argument about attention, not an aesthetic.

## Material

- Rams's ten principles include "good design is as little design as possible".
- The SK4 radio and the 606 shelving system are the usual examples.
- The talk should end with a question for the room, not a summary slide.

## Visual constraint

Light background, dark text, large display type. No other direction given yet.
