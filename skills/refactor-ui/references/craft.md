# Craft: make it look intentional

"Ugly" and "cheap" usually come from broken relationships, not missing effects. Fix the relationships first; add expression last, as one decisive move.

Read this file when the complaint is that the work looks ugly, cheap, unfinished, plain, or generic.

## 1. Diagnose in repair order

Later fixes cannot rescue earlier failures. Check in this order:

1. alignment and edges;
2. spacing and grouping;
3. type scale, weight, and line length;
4. color roles and contrast;
5. depth — borders and shadows — and shape;
6. imagery and icons;
7. one expressive move.

If the work fails steps 1–3, adding effects from 5–7 makes it worse.

## 2. Type

- Use a small scale: three or four sizes and two or three weights. Avoid arbitrary values.
- Body line length is roughly 45–75 characters. Line-height is roughly 1.5 for body and 1.2 for headings.
- Left-align long text; center only short display lines.
- Use weight and color for hierarchy before adding another size.
- Do not use faux bold or faux italic.
- For CJK text: no uppercase, no negative tracking, line-height around 1.7–1.9, and real bold weights and italics.

## 3. Color

- Start from a neutral base, one accent, and semantic colors for success, warning, and error.
- Reserve the accent for the primary action or the one thing that should pop. If it appears everywhere, it means nothing.
- Body text needs about 4.5:1 contrast; large text and UI boundaries about 3:1.
- Avoid pure black on white and pure white on black. Tint the grays warm or cool to match the work.
- Do not rely on color alone to carry state; pair it with a label, icon, or shape.
- Dark mode is its own set of surface and text roles, not an inversion.

## 4. Space

- Proximity communicates grouping: space inside a group should be smaller than space between groups.
- Use one spacing scale and consistent padding. Align to shared edges.
- Give the focal element room. Do not stretch content edge to edge just because the viewport is wide.
- Generous space reads as confidence. Cramped space reads as cheap. Empty space without hierarchy reads as unfinished.

## 5. Depth and shape

- Pick one depth cue: borders, background shifts, or shadows. Not all three on every element.
- Shadows express elevation. Keep one light direction. If everything floats, nothing does.
- Keep radii and border weights consistent. A single sharp corner can be a deliberate accent, but it should be one.

## 6. Images and icons

- Crop to the subject. Do not stretch. Keep a consistent ratio or mat.
- Keep the caption near the image. Give credits a stable, quiet style.
- Match icon stroke weight and size to the type. Do not use an icon to repeat a label.
- The image must show what the text claims. Open the actual file and compare it with the claim, caption, and alt text. A file that loads is not evidence.

## 7. When it looks plain or unfinished

Decide which case you have:

- **Weak hierarchy, contrast, or scale.** Fix those first; the work may not need more decoration.
- **No point of view.** Add one decisive move drawn from the content: a type treatment, a color field, an image scale, a diagram, or a spatial rhythm.
- **Missing craft signals.** Tighten alignment, spacing, and type before adding ornament.

One decisive move beats ten small decorations. Do not confuse "more" with "designed", and do not confuse "quiet" with "unfinished".

## 8. Completion checks

- Squint: the intended hierarchy survives.
- At real size: no misalignment, clipped text, stretched image, or inconsistent spacing.
- Contrast passes, and state is not carried by color alone.
- Any expressive decision is specific to this content.
- The work looks intentional at its actual viewing size, not only in code.

## Avoid

- Gradient, shadow, and large radius on everything.
- Many font sizes, families, or accent colors.
- Centered long text and weak alignment.
- Pure black text, stretched images, decorative icons.
- "Premium" effects with no job.
- Adding decoration before the spacing and type are sound.
